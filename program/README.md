# Microsoft for Startups program navigator

Program terms and portal experiences change. This section routes founders to
current official information without copying volatile values into the guide.

> **Verified in the August 16, 2026 repository audit against current official
> Microsoft documentation.**

## Choose the experience that applies to you

Microsoft currently documents two experiences based on when the startup joined:

| Enrollment date | Current documented experience |
| --- | --- |
| Before May 29, 2026 | Use the [Microsoft for Startups portal](https://portal.startups.microsoft.com/login) for the applicable legacy benefit and advisor workflows |
| On or after May 29, 2026 | Use the startup homepage in the [Azure portal](https://portal.azure.com/) and the **Your Microsoft team** section |

Use the current
[activation guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/startups/build/azure-getting-started/activate)
if the interface differs. The legacy experience is still documented, but the
program is actively evolving.

The application flow can also differ based on whether you have an investor
referral code. Follow the current application workflow and do not infer credit
amounts or eligibility from an older screenshot, copied checklist, or blog post.

## Important account model

The credit offer is activated by an individual using the Microsoft account
required by the program. After activation, complete the company identity and
administration work described in the
[first 48 hours journey](https://github.com/Azure-Startup-Field-Guide/guide/tree/main/journeys/first-48-hours).

## Avoid hard-coded assumptions

Before making a business decision, verify:

- current eligibility rules;
- available credit amount and duration;
- which services and marketplace offers are covered;
- whether a payment method is required;
- the transition behavior when credits expire;
- the sponsorship end date and current remaining balance;
- regional availability and quota; and
- the support experience for your enrollment date.

This guide intentionally links to current program pages instead of promising a
specific benefit level.

## Plan for graduation from the beginning

The program and its credits are time-bound. Current Microsoft documentation
states that remaining credits expire at graduation and the sponsored
subscription transitions to standard pay-as-you-go while Azure access
continues.

Before the first production workload:

1. record the sponsorship end date and billing owner;
2. estimate the workload at public pay-as-you-go rates;
3. identify resources that must be resized or removed before credits end; and
4. choose the support path the company will use after program benefits end.

Do not wait for the first pay-as-you-go invoice to test whether the product has
sustainable unit economics.
