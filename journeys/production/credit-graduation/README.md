# Prepare for startup-credit graduation

> **Goal:** move from credits to standard billing without service interruption or
> an avoidable cost shock.

Program terms change. Use the current Microsoft for Startups terms for your
specific offer as the source of truth.

## Start before the balance reaches zero

1. Confirm the credit expiration date and remaining balance.
2. Confirm a valid payment method and billing ownership.
3. Forecast the first months of standard billing using recent actual usage.
4. Remove idle resources and rightsize the stable workload.
5. Create budgets, anomaly alerts, and cost-allocation tags.
6. Review reservations or savings plans only after measuring the stable
   baseline.
7. Confirm the post-program support path.
8. Record the transition owner, date, and rollback or spending guardrail.

Graduation guidance states that the Azure subscription continues and moves to
standard pay-as-you-go billing after program credits expire. Remaining credits
expire, and program-specific benefits conclude.

## Common failure modes

- treating the portal credit balance as a forecast;
- waiting until the final week to add a payment method;
- committing to reserved usage before rightsizing;
- assuming credits can be extended or transferred;
- leaving experiments in production subscriptions; and
- losing program support without selecting the next support route.

## Weekly transition dashboard

Track:

- remaining credit and expiration date;
- current daily and monthly burn;
- forecast at graduation;
- largest services and unexpected movers;
- optimization actions and verified savings;
- payment-method readiness; and
- open quota, support, or architecture risks.
